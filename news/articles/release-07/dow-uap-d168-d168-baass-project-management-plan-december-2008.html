Release 07 / feature investigation / document analysis

Roswell’s Egg-Shaped Craft and the Bodies Under Canvas

A 2002 sealed affidavit preserved inside a 2008 BAASS project file describes an alleged recovered object, apparent occupants, and a temporary morgue. If independently corroborated, it would be one of the most consequential claims in the modern UFO record. The affidavit itself, however, is not the same thing as a contemporaneous 1947 recovery report.

Document at a glance
Source file: DOW-UAP-D168-BAASS_Project_Management_Plan-December_2008.pdf
Key material: Appendix 2, reproduced 2002 sealed affidavit
Claimed setting: Roswell Army Air Base, Building 84 / Hangar P-3
Claimed date of events: July 1947
Evidence label: retrospective sworn account preserved in a later contractor archive

The detail that changes the story

Most UFO reports begin with something distant and ambiguous: a light, a shape in the sky, a radar return, or a witness trying to estimate motion and size from far away. The Roswell account preserved here is different in kind. It describes an alleged object at close range, inside a military hangar, after recovery.

According to the reproduced affidavit, the witness was taken to Building 84—identified in the document as Hangar P-3—and saw an object recovered north of Roswell. The description is unusually concrete: approximately 12 to 15 feet long, about six feet high, somewhat narrower than its length, and “more of an egg shape.” The surface appeared metallic. The statement says there were no visible windows, portholes, wings, tail section, or landing gear.

“12 to 15 feet in length…about 6 feet high, and more of an egg shape.”

That is the part UFO enthusiasts are right to notice. This is not simply a claim that somebody saw something unusual over the desert. It is a claim of a recovered vehicle-like object whose form did not resemble a conventional aircraft, followed by a claim that apparent occupants were present at the same recovery site.

The bodies and the temporary morgue

The affidavit says the witness could also see a couple of bodies beneath a canvas tarpaulin. Only the heads extended beyond the covering, and the witness said he could not make out their features. The account further says the witness was informed that a temporary morgue had been established for recovered bodies.

That combination—an unusual enclosed object, apparent bodies, and a temporary morgue—is why this document has a significance far beyond an ordinary sighting report. If the account were corroborated by independent 1947 records or witnesses, it would point toward an alleged physical-recovery event involving both technology and occupants. That would move the Roswell story from the category of “unidentified debris” into the much more consequential category of alleged non-human vehicle recovery.

What the affidavit claims
  • An object approximately 12–15 feet long and six feet high.
  • An egg-shaped, apparently metallic form.
  • No visible wings, tail, landing gear, windows, or portholes.
  • Two apparent bodies under a canvas covering.
  • A temporary morgue established for recovered bodies.
  • Wreckage reportedly described to the witness as not radioactive.

Why this matters to the disclosure story

For a UFO researcher, the importance is not merely that the claim is dramatic. It is that the alleged event contains several categories of evidence that could, in principle, leave independent traces: a recovery location, military personnel, a hangar, transportation, medical handling, a morgue, chain-of-custody decisions, and a public-information response. A claim with that many potential records is testable in a way that a single distant observation is not.

It also explains why Roswell remains central to the disclosure community. The enduring question is not only “what did someone see?” It is “did a government organization recover something, and did multiple offices subsequently manage the object and its occupants?” The affidavit addresses that larger question directly—but only as a later witness account.

What the document establishes—and what it does not

The document establishes that a 2002 affidavit containing these allegations was reproduced and preserved in a 2008 BAASS project-management file. It does not, by itself, authenticate the 1947 events. The affidavit is retrospective, roughly 55 years after the alleged recovery, and the reproduced pages contain redactions. The account also includes information the witness says he was told, which is different from what he personally observed.

That distinction matters. The apparent object description is presented as an observation. The temporary-morgue detail is described as information given to the witness. The conclusion that the object was an extraterrestrial craft and the bodies were extraterrestrial occupants is an interpretation. Those layers should not be merged into one undifferentiated assertion.

The enthusiast’s reading, without pretending certainty

From a UFO-enthusiast perspective, this is exactly the kind of document that deserves attention. It describes a physical object with an unusual geometry, apparent occupants, and military handling—far more specific than the average UAP report. It is reasonable to say that, if true, the account would be extraordinary and historically explosive.

But “extraordinary” does not mean “already proven.” A later affidavit can preserve a genuine memory, a distorted memory, a story passed through several people, or a deliberate fabrication. The correct response is not to ignore it because it is sensational, nor to promote it as settled fact because it is sworn. The correct response is to identify what would corroborate or weaken it.

What could confirm or falsify the account?

  • Contemporaneous 1947 records identifying Building 84, recovery teams, or unusual medical and transport activity.
  • Independent witnesses who knew about the object or bodies without relying on this affidavit.
  • Personnel, hospital, morgue, burial, logistics, or chain-of-custody records matching the account.
  • Unredacted originals, provenance records, and a clear explanation of how the affidavit entered the BAASS file.
  • Physical evidence that can be independently tested and linked to the alleged event.

Bottom line

This is one of the most significant claims in Release 07—not because the archive has proved that aliens were recovered at Roswell, but because the affidavit describes the kind of event that, if independently verified, would transform the case. An egg-shaped object in a military hangar, apparent bodies under canvas, and a temporary morgue is a claim of enormous consequence.

The document deserves to be read as a serious lead and a historically important record of what was alleged. It does not deserve to be reduced to a generic “unidentified object” summary, and it does not yet deserve to be presented as authenticated proof. The unresolved gap between those two positions is precisely where the investigation belongs.
